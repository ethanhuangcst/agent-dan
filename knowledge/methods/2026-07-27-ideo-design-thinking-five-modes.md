# IDEO / d.school design thinking — five modes

Human-centered design thinking for innovation work. This note treats the **Stanford d.school five modes** as the primary mental model (what many IDEO-adjacent courses teach), with IDEO’s **seven-phase** public framing as an alternate shared language on [designthinking.ideo.com](https://designthinking.ideo.com/).

## Five modes (canonical for this KB)

Non-linear and iterative — teams loop back after testing, especially into Empathize and Define.

| Mode | Purpose | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Empathize** | Understand people you design for | Observation, interviews, immersion in context |
| **Define** | Turn research into direction | Synthesize into a human-centered **point of view** / problem statement (pain, need, or opportunity — not a company wish list) |
| **Ideate** | Expand the solution space | Many diverse ideas before converging; defer early judgment |
| **Prototype** | Learn by making | Low-cost, tangible artifacts (sketches, flows, mockups, role-play) |
| **Test** | Learn from real use | Feedback on prototypes; refine solutions and assumptions |

### IDEO lineage

- IDEO has practiced human-centered design since 1978 and helped popularize design thinking in organizations ([introduction](https://designthinking.ideo.com/introduction)).
- David Kelley (IDEO) co-founded the **Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (d.school)**, which formalized the five **modes** in teaching materials such as the [Design Thinking Bootleg](https://dschool.stanford.edu/resources/design-thinking-bootleg) (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test).

### Three lenses (IDEO)

When evaluating solutions, balance ([introduction](https://designthinking.ideo.com/introduction)):

- **Desirability** — Does it matter to people?
- **Viability** — Can it succeed in real constraints?
- **Feasibility** — Can we build it with current capabilities?

Human-centered design is the **orientation** (start with people); design thinking is the **methods** (explore, ideate, prototype, iterate).

## IDEO seven phases (alternate packaging)

IDEO’s current process site describes **seven non-linear phases** and a rhythm of **creating choices** (diverge) and **making choices** (converge) ([process](https://designthinking.ideo.com/process)):

1. Frame the question (often “How might we…?”)
2. Gather inspiration
3. Synthesize for action
4. Generate ideas
5. Make ideas tangible
6. Test to learn
7. Share the story

**Rough crosswalk to five modes** (heuristic, not an official IDEO mapping):

| Five modes | Seven phases (approx.) |
| --- | --- |
| Empathize | Gather inspiration; parts of Frame the question |
| Define | Synthesize for action; parts of Frame the question |
| Ideate | Generate ideas |
| Prototype | Make ideas tangible |
| Test | Test to learn |
| — | Share the story (communication / alignment; no dedicated fifth-mode step) |

## When to use

Especially useful under uncertainty, complex systems, rapid technology change, and multi-stakeholder problems ([introduction](https://designthinking.ideo.com/introduction)).
